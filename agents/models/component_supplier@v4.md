# Thesis: memory, storage, networking, optics and servers (component_supplier@v4)

## Thesis

These companies sell the parts and boxes around the GPU: memory, drives, switches, optics, servers and the contract manufacturing that assembles them. They are paid by the buildout, so revenue exposure is the measure that matters. Capex allocation matters for one company, Micron, whose fab and HBM capacity is the one supplier capex that is partly AI and said to be: Micron spent $30.7 billion gross ($27.4 billion net of incentives) in fiscal 2026 and guides about $25 billion net for the first half of fiscal 2027 alone, "higher in the second half". Project-level investment matters for the fab builders (Micron) and the factory builders (Supermicro, Dell, the contract manufacturers); plans versus reality matters for Micron's capex guidance. The signal to watch is the data-center or AI line of revenue where the company names one (Dell's AI-optimized servers, Sandisk's Datacenter, Coherent's Datacenter & Communications, Fabrinet's Data center, Cisco's hyperscaler AI orders) and the capex Micron commits to HBM and DRAM.

## Where the AI money shows up

1. **Segment or market revenue** (`facts.segment_revenue`): Micron CMBU and CDBU; Sandisk Datacenter; Coherent Datacenter & Communications; Fabrinet Data center; AAOI Datacenter; Dell ISG and its AI-optimized servers line; HPE Server; Celestica CCS and HPS; Jabil Intelligent Infrastructure; Flex Cloud and Power Infrastructure; Penguin Advanced Computing; Arista Product; Cisco Networking. Seller side of the buildout.
2. **Capex** (`values.capex`): Micron's fab and HBM capacity is the one supplier capex that is AI-driven in part; Micron states a first-half fiscal 2027 figure tied to HBM and DRAM but no AI share. Coherent's capex more than doubled for indium phosphide capacity.
3. **What the company says** (`ai_spend`): AI server revenue and orders (Dell, Supermicro on calls, HPE orders), hyperscaler AI infrastructure orders and revenue (Cisco), AI-related revenue (Jabil on calls), AI networking goals (Arista), datacenter revenue (Coherent, Sandisk, AAOI, Fabrinet).

## How to attribute

Capex and opex:

- Micron: `stated_share` or `stated_amount` if the company gives the HBM or AI share of capex; it has not, as of the fiscal 2026 10-K. Otherwise `qualitative` with confidence ≤ 0.4 ("capex to support HBM- and DRAM-related investments").
- Arista, Cisco: `none` for capex (their capex is small and not AI); `qualitative` or `none` for opex.
- Coherent: `qualitative` ≤ 0.4 for capex while the expansion is stated as indium phosphide for AI datacenters with no amount split.
- Every other company here: `none` for capex unless an expansion is stated as being for AI demand with an amount; `qualitative` or `none` for opex.
- As with chip sellers, record data-center or AI revenue in `basis` as the seller-side signal, never in `ai_amount`; the revenue field below is where it becomes a row.

Revenue, in order of hardness:

- `stated_share`: the company states AI's share of revenue. Supermicro states the share from AI solutions on calls (about 60% in Q4 fiscal 2026); use it. Confidence ≤ 0.9 for a filing, ≤ 0.7 for a call figure.
- `stated_amount`: the company states AI revenue for the year: Dell's AI-optimized server revenue ($24.7 billion fiscal 2026), Cisco's hyperscaler AI infrastructure revenue (about $4 billion fiscal 2026), Jabil's AI-related revenue ($14.4 billion fiscal 2026, call). Orders and backlog are not revenue: confidence ≤ 0.5 for an orders figure (Dell's $64 billion, HPE's $6.8 billion, Cisco's $9.3 billion) and say so. Confidence ≤ 0.9 for a revenue figure in a release, ≤ 0.7 from a call.
- `segment`: sum of the `segment_revenue` facts that are the data-center line, named per company. Micron CMBU from fiscal 2023, when the segment was created (fiscal 2022 and before had no cloud segment: "Compute and Networking" includes client and graphics and is not an AI line, so those years are `qualitative` or `none`, never CNBU); CDBU is data center too but includes enterprise storage and non-AI DRAM, ≤ 0.5. Sandisk Datacenter, AAOI Datacenter, Fabrinet Data center: ≤ 0.6. Coherent Datacenter & Communications: ≤ 0.5 (communications is telecom). Dell "Infrastructure Solutions Group" and HPE "Server" are data-center lines that include non-AI servers: ≤ 0.5. Celestica CCS or HPS, Jabil Intelligent Infrastructure, Flex Cloud and Power Infrastructure, Penguin Advanced Computing: ≤ 0.6. Arista and Cisco have no AI segment; do not use "Product" or "Networking" as one. Lumentum has no segment since fiscal 2026; Components and Systems are product lines, not markets.
- `qualitative`: revenue growth attributed to AI or cloud demand in words only, confidence ≤ 0.3. The answer for Western Digital, Seagate, NetApp and Ciena (cloud provider share is a customer mix, not an AI line).
- `none`: no link stated.
- `whole_business`: never, even for Supermicro; use its stated share.

## Projects

A project is a fab or factory with a location and an amount: Micron's fabs (Idaho, New York, Singapore, Japan) and HBM packaging capacity, Supermicro's and Dell's plant expansions, Coherent's indium phosphide capacity, a contract manufacturer's new site stated as being for AI or data-center customers. Words that name one: "fab", "facility", "campus", "expansion", "investment of", "capacity", "square feet" with a location. Not a project: a product launch (a new drive, switch or server), a customer win, an order figure, a spin-off, a hire.

## Traps

- A memory upcycle lifts total revenue; only the AI/HBM part is attributable, and only when stated. Micron's fiscal 2026 revenue more than tripled; the 10-K gives a data-center share (about 60%), not an AI one.
- Orders, bookings and backlog are not revenue; a stated AI order figure gets a low ceiling and the word "orders" in `basis`. Cisco states both orders ($9.3 billion) and revenue (about $4 billion) for the same year; take the revenue.
- A server segment (Dell ISG, HPE Server) is a data-center line, not an AI one; cap the confidence. Dell's AI-optimized servers line is the AI one.
- Micron renamed its segments in fiscal 2025; an older "Compute and Networking" fact is not comparable to "Cloud Memory".
- A call figure (Supermicro's 60%, Jabil's $14.4 billion, Arista's 48% titans) is not in the filing; cite the call and cap the confidence.
- Cloud end markets (Western Digital's almost 90%, Ciena's 53%) are customer groups, not AI lines.
