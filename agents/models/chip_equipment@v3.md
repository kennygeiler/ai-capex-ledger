# Thesis: chip equipment and materials (chip_equipment@v3)

## Thesis

Equipment makers are one step upstream of the chips. They are paid by foundry and memory capex, which is itself pulled by AI demand for leading-edge logic, HBM and advanced packaging. SEMI put 300mm fab equipment spending at $133 billion for 2026 (up 18 percent) and $151 billion for 2027, and credited AI chip demand and HBM (SEMI, 1 Apr 2026). Revenue exposure is the measure that matters, and it is only as good as what the company states, because end-market lines (foundry, memory, DRAM) are broader than AI. Capex allocation does not matter: their own capex is tool factories, labs and R&D sites, and none of the eight states an AI part of it. Project-level investment matters a little (plant and R&D expansions); plans versus reality does not. The signal to watch is the stated AI, HBM or advanced-packaging share or amount of revenue, and behind it the capex of TSMC, Samsung, Micron and SK hynix, which the ledger does not hold. TrendForce put DRAM makers' capex at $53.7 billion for 2025 and $61.3 billion for 2026, aimed at HBM and process upgrades rather than new capacity (TechNews citing TrendForce, 14 Nov 2025).

## Companies

Lam Research (LRCX). No AI segment or line. The FY2026 10-K (year ended 28 June 2026) splits revenue into "Systems revenue" $14,885 million and "Customer support-related revenue and other" $8,347 million, and gives system revenue by market: foundry 54 percent, memory 39 percent, logic/integrated device manufacturing 7 percent. It does not split memory into DRAM and NAND and gives no AI, HBM or advanced-packaging figure; AI appears as "a significant driver of capital equipment expenditures" in words only. Capex was $966.4 million "to support lab investments in the United States and global growth in manufacturing facilities". In the layer because it sells deposition and etch tools to the foundries and memory makers whose capex the AI buildout pulls.

Applied Materials (AMAT). No AI segment. Segments in the Q3 FY2026 release (quarter ended 26 July 2026): "Semiconductor Systems" $7,040 million, "Applied Global Services" $1,781 million, "Other, which includes Display" $294 million. Semiconductor Systems by end market: foundry, logic and other 67 percent, DRAM 26 percent, flash memory 7 percent. No AI dollar figure. Stated growth figures: advanced packaging revenue expected to grow more than 70 percent in 2026 (raised from more than 50 percent), Semiconductor Systems more than 30 percent, process diagnostics and control more than 50 percent (TrendForce News on the 13 Aug 2026 call). Capex $1,988 million for the nine months of FY2026. In the layer as the broadest tool maker; its DRAM and advanced-packaging lines are the closest it gets to an AI line.

KLA (KLAC). No AI segment. Segments in the Q4 FY2026 release (year ended 30 June 2026): "Semiconductor Process Control" $12,245 million, "Specialty Semiconductor Process" $584 million, "PCB and Component Inspection" $750 million; total $13,579 million. The release gives no AI, HBM or advanced-packaging figure; it says "the AI infrastructure buildout is also driving new growth opportunities in advanced packaging". On the call the company said advanced-packaging process-control revenue should be about $1.1 billion in calendar 2026, up more than 70 percent (TIKR summary, 29 Jul 2026). Capex $375.9 million for FY2026. In the layer because process control is paid out of the same fab capex.

Teradyne (TER). Segment "Semiconductor Test" is the AI-exposed line: $1,111 million of $1,282 million in Q1 2026 (quarter ended 29 Mar 2026), $1,122 million of $1,329 million in Q2 2026 (quarter ended 28 Jun 2026). Stated share: "approximately 70% of our revenue tied to AI-related demand" (Q1 2026 release, 28 Apr 2026). The Q2 2026 release states no share; it says "Q3 guidance reflects robust AI-related demand". Purchases of PP&E $155.4 million for the first half of 2026. In the layer because it tests the accelerators and HBM the buildout buys, and it states an AI share.

Entegris (ENTG). No AI segment or line. Segments in the Q2 2026 release (quarter ended 27 Jun 2026): "Materials Solutions" $371.3 million, "Advanced Purity Solutions" $514.6 million; net sales $883.2 million. No AI, HBM or advanced-packaging figure as of 2026-10-29; "artificial intelligence" appears only as a risk factor. Capex $80.8 million for the first half. At the edge: it sells materials and filtration by wafer volume, so its AI exposure is a demand statement, not a line.

Onto Innovation (ONTO). No AI segment. Markets named in the Q2 2026 release (quarter ended 30 Jun 2026): "Advanced Nodes" (up 50 percent sequentially to a record), "Specialty Devices and Advanced Packaging" (a record); revenue $343.1 million; no dollar figures per market. Stated on the call: full-year 2026 advanced-packaging growth outlook raised to approximately 80 percent, demand "strongest from HBM manufacturers and OSAT", advanced packaging and specialty devices "nearly half of the revenue in Q2", backlog above $1.1 billion. Capex not disclosed in the release as of 2026-10-29. In the layer because its inspection and metrology sells into HBM and 2.5D packaging lines.

Advanced Energy (AEIS). The one company here with a real data-center line. Markets in the Q2 2026 release (quarter ended 30 Jun 2026): "Semiconductor Equipment" $278.3 million, "Data Center Computing" $191.5 million (Q2 2025: $141.6 million), "Industrial and Medical" $80.0 million, "Telecom and Networking" $24.3 million. Q1 2026: Data Center Computing $194.2 million, up from $96.2 million. Stated outlook: data-center revenue "more than 50% in 2026 after more than doubling in 2025", driven by "increased demand from hyperscalers", four hyperscale customers (Q2 2026 call, 3 Aug 2026). Capex: $50 million in Q2, 2026 capex guided to $180 million to $195 million for factory capacity, not stated as AI. In the layer for its semiconductor power subsystems; its Data Center Computing line is a server power-supply business sold to hyperscalers and is the AI-exposed line.

Axcelis (ACLS). No AI segment or line. The Q2 2026 release (quarter ended 30 Jun 2026) reports "Product" $200.5 million and "Services" $14.7 million of $215.2 million, discusses Power, Memory and General Mature markets in words, mentions "growing end-demand in data center" and "Demand in the Memory market remains robust", and does not use the word AI. No AI or HBM figure as of 2026-10-29. Capex $5.4 million for the first half. At the edge: implant tools sold mostly to power and mature-node fabs. A merger with Veeco was pending at the Q2 release.

The foundries and lithography (TSMC, ASML) that pay these companies file no 10-K and are out of the set.

## Where the AI money shows up

1. **Revenue by end market or segment** (`facts.segment_revenue`): Lam "Systems" and its foundry, memory and logic percentages; Applied "Semiconductor Systems" with its foundry/logic, DRAM and flash percentages; KLA "Semiconductor Process Control"; Teradyne "Semiconductor Test"; Advanced Energy "Data Center Computing" and "Semiconductor Equipment"; Entegris "Materials Solutions" and "Advanced Purity Solutions". Seller-side signal only, except Advanced Energy's data-center line, which is a product line sold to hyperscalers.
2. **What the company says** (`ai_spend`): the quantified statements are Teradyne's "approximately 70% of our revenue tied to AI-related demand" (Q1 2026), Applied's advanced-packaging growth of more than 70 percent in 2026, KLA's about $1.1 billion of advanced-packaging process-control revenue in calendar 2026, Onto's approximately 80 percent advanced-packaging growth in 2026, Advanced Energy's at least 50 percent data-center growth in 2026. Lam, Entegris and Axcelis give words only.
3. **Capex** (`values.capex`): tool factories, labs and R&D fabs (Lam $966.4 million FY2026; Applied $1,988 million for nine months FY2026; KLA $375.9 million FY2026; Advanced Energy $180 million to $195 million guided for 2026). Not AI spend unless the company says a line of capex is for AI-driven demand, which none of the eight does as of 2026-10-29.

## How to attribute

Capex and opex:

- `stated_amount` / `stated_share` only when the company states an AI part of its own spend (`scope: ai`). None of the eight does as of 2026-10-29.
- Otherwise `none` for capex and opex. Record the AI, HBM or advanced-packaging share of revenue in `basis` as the seller-side signal, never in `ai_amount`; the revenue field below is where it becomes a row.

Revenue, in order of hardness:

- `stated_share`: the company states AI's, HBM's or advanced packaging's share of systems or total revenue; confidence ≤ 0.8, lower (≤ 0.6) when the statement is about orders or shipments rather than revenue. Teradyne's approximately 70 percent (Q1 2026) is the example; Onto's "nearly half" from advanced packaging and specialty devices is a share of a broader line, ≤ 0.5.
- `stated_amount`: the company states AI-related or advanced-packaging revenue for the year; confidence ≤ 0.8. KLA's about $1.1 billion of advanced-packaging process-control revenue for calendar 2026 is an outlook figure, ≤ 0.5 until reported.
- `segment`: not allowed for "Foundry", "Memory", "DRAM", "Systems", "Semiconductor Systems", "Semiconductor Process Control" or "Semiconductor Test"; they are end markets broader than AI, and a DRAM line is not an HBM line. Record them in `basis` only. One exception: Advanced Energy's "Data Center Computing" is a product line sold to hyperscalers for AI servers; `segment` with confidence ≤ 0.6, with `basis` noting it includes non-AI server power.
- `qualitative`: AI named as the driver of demand with no figure, confidence ≤ 0.3. The answer for Lam, Entegris and Axcelis as of 2026-10-29.
- `none`: no link stated.
- `whole_business`: never.

## Projects

A project is a plant or R&D site expansion with a location and an amount: a new tool factory (Advanced Energy's Thailand factory), a research center, a packaging-development line. Words that name one: "facility", "campus", "expansion", "square feet", "investment of", "center" with a location. Not a project: a tool launch (Applied's six new DRAM and packaging systems), a customer's fab (the customer's project, not this company's), an order, a hire, a merger.

## Traps

- "AI drove N% of our revenue" is a demand statement, not the company's spend. It belongs in `basis` for capex and in the revenue field as `stated_share`.
- Equipment capex cycles follow the foundries and memory makers, not this company's AI decisions.
- A share of orders, shipments or backlog is not a share of revenue; cap the confidence and say which it was. Onto's backlog above $1.1 billion is backlog.
- A growth rate ("advanced packaging up more than 70 percent") is not a share or an amount; it belongs in `basis` unless the base amount is also stated.
- A customer's fab announcement (TSMC Arizona, Micron Idaho) is not this company's project.
- Applied's DRAM share of Semiconductor Systems (26 percent in Q3 FY2026) is a DRAM line; HBM is part of it, not all of it.
- Advanced Energy's data-center line is the one place in this layer where `segment` is allowed; do not extend that to its Semiconductor Equipment line.
