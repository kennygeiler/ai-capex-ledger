# Thesis: bitcoin miner turned HPC / AI host (miner_to_hpc@v3)

## Thesis

These companies own cheap power and sites, and they are turning them from bitcoin mining into GPU hosting or AI cloud. Two businesses share one balance sheet, and only the HPC or AI segment is AI spend. Capex allocation (segment capex), revenue exposure (segment revenue) and project-level investment (a named campus converted under a lease with a hyperscaler or neocloud) all matter; plans versus reality matters because HPC capex guidance is large relative to the company and often slips. The signal to watch is the HPC segment's capex against its revenue, year by year: it shows whether the conversion is earning yet, and the mining segment is noise.

The facts as of 29 Oct 2026 show how far apart the two numbers sit. Applied Digital spent $2,988.7m of capex on its HPC Hosting segment in fiscal 2026 against $385.3m of segment revenue. IREN spent $4.3bn on plant, equipment and computer hardware in fiscal 2026 against $128.8m of AI Cloud revenue, with $13.8bn of capex commitments due within a year. The leases behind the capex are long and large ($7.0bn to $12.8bn per company over 10 to 25 years) and are usually backstopped by Google, so the contract value, not the revenue, is what the market prices. Both numbers belong in the dataset, in different fields.

## Where the AI money shows up

1. **Segment capex** (`facts.segment_capex`, ASC 280) is the hard number: the HPC / AI cloud / colocation segment's capital expenditures. Applied Digital is the one company that reports it (HPC Hosting $2,988.7m in fiscal 2026). IREN, Core Scientific, TeraWulf, Hut 8 and Galaxy report segment revenue but consolidated capex only.
2. **Segment revenue** (`facts.segment_revenue`) for the same segment shows whether the capex is already earning: IREN "AI Cloud", Applied Digital "HPC Hosting Business", Core Scientific "Colocation", TeraWulf "HPC Leasing", Galaxy "Data Centers", Bit Digital "cloud services" and "colocation services", Soluna "High-Performance Computing Services".
3. **What the company says** (`ai_spend`): GPU purchases with counts and amounts (Bit Digital's 130 H200 servers for about $30m), per-MW build costs (Cipher's $9m to $11m per critical IT MW), capex guidance (IREN's $25bn to $30bn for fiscal 2027), capex commitments (IREN's $13.8bn at 30 June 2026).
4. **Finance leases, customer-funded capex and backstops**: CoreWeave funded $226.2m of Core Scientific's $279.2m Q4 2025 capex; Google backstops Fluidstack's leases at TeraWulf, Cipher and Hut 8 and holds warrants in return. **Purchase commitments** cover contracted GPUs and construction.

## How to attribute

Capex, in order of hardness:

- `segment`: `ai_amount = segment capex` of the AI/HPC segment(s) for the year. If several segments are AI/HPC (hosting plus cloud), add them and list every fact used. As of the FY2025/FY2026 filings this works for Applied Digital only.
- `stated_amount`: an explicit AI amount from a filing or release when no segment capex exists for that year (a GPU purchase with a dollar figure, a stated HPC build spend).
- `ppe_category`: growth in a GPU/HPC-specific PP&E category, when the note separates it.
- `qualitative`: consolidated capex said to be mostly HPC (TeraWulf's $1.06bn in 2025, Galaxy's $1.19bn in 2025) with no segment split: no amount, confidence ≤ 0.3, name the consolidated figure in `basis`.
- `none` otherwise, including every year a company reports mining only (Cipher through 2025, Riot, MARA, CleanSpark).

Opex: `segment` cost of revenue for the AI segment when the filing states it (IREN AI Cloud cost of revenue $16.9m in fiscal 2026, TeraWulf HPC Leasing cost of revenue $2.5m in 2025); otherwise `none`.

Revenue, in order of hardness:

- `stated_amount` / `stated_share`: when the company states HPC or AI revenue for the year outside the segment note; confidence ≤ 0.9. Bitdeer's AI cloud revenue of $6.8m in 2025 is this kind.
- `segment`: sum of the `segment_revenue` facts for the HPC / AI segment(s): IREN "AI Cloud", Applied Digital "HPC Hosting Business", Core Scientific "Colocation", TeraWulf "HPC Leasing", Galaxy "Data Centers", Bit Digital "cloud services" plus "colocation services", Soluna "High-Performance Computing Services". Name every segment used. Confidence ≤ 0.8. Colocation revenue from a hyperscaler or neocloud tenant counts: the lease is AI hosting even though the GPUs are the tenant's.
- `qualitative`: the company links revenue growth to HPC in words only, confidence ≤ 0.3.
- `none`: a year with only mining revenue, or with a segment total and no HPC line. Hut 8's Compute segment is mining plus AI cloud with no split: `none`, with the segment named in `basis`. MARA's hosting services line is miner hosting: `none`. Soluna's Data Center Hosting is bitcoin hosting: `none`.
- `whole_business`: never; the mining segment is always there.

## Projects

A project is a named site converted or built for HPC with a counterparty, megawatts or an amount: a campus lease with a hyperscaler or neocloud (Applied Digital's Polaris Forge 1 at Ellendale leased to CoreWeave, 400 MW, about $11.0bn; Core Scientific's sites contracted to CoreWeave, about 590 MW; TeraWulf's Lake Mariner and Abernathy leased to Fluidstack, 522 MW, over $12.8bn; Cipher's Barber Lake to Fluidstack and Black Pearl to AWS, 600 MW; Hut 8's River Bend to Fluidstack, 245 MW, $7.0bn; Galaxy's Helios to CoreWeave, 800 MW; IREN's Horizon at Childress for Microsoft, $9.7bn; Riot's Rockdale lease to AMD, 25 MW), a GPU purchase with a count and amount, a financing tied to a site (Galaxy's $1.4bn Helios facility, Cipher's senior secured notes for Barber Lake), a joint venture with a stake and a site (TeraWulf's 50.1% of Abernathy, MARA's Starwood venture). Words that name one: "campus", "lease", "critical IT load", "megawatts", "gigawatt", "hosting agreement", "GPUs", "energized", "phase", "backstop", "rent commencement". Not a project: a hashrate target, an ASIC purchase, a mining site expansion, a new power contract with no HPC use, a hire.

## Traps

- Mining capex (ASICs, "miners", "mining equipment", "deposits on equipment" for miners) is not AI. Never include the mining segment. Riot paid about $228.4m for miners in 2025; none of it is AI.
- Colocation shells leased to a hyperscaler are AI infrastructure spend by this company even though the GPUs are the tenant's; count the segment capex, say in `basis` that the GPUs are the tenant's. When the tenant funds the build (CoreWeave at Core Scientific), say so; the capex is still this company's.
- A year with a segment total but no AI segment line (before the pivot) is `none`, not zero. Cipher, Riot, MARA and CleanSpark are `none` for 2025 with large project rows.
- A lease's total contract value ("$7.0 billion over 15 years", "over $12.8 billion") is a project amount, not revenue in the year. Contracted ARR (IREN's $4bn) is a run-rate, not a fiscal-year figure.
- Bitcoin price lifts mining revenue and total revenue together; only the HPC segment is attributable.
- A segment that mixes mining and AI cloud (Hut 8's Compute) cannot be attributed by segment; wait for the split.
- Hosting revenue is usually miner hosting (MARA, Soluna's Data Center Hosting, Applied Digital's Data Center Hosting Business), not AI hosting; read the segment definition before counting it.
- A per-MW cost estimate times contracted MW is a reader's calculation, not a stated amount; it goes in `basis`, not `ai_amount`.
- Bitdeer files 20-F; its years are a gap for the pipeline. Bit Digital's cloud business sits in a consolidated subsidiary (WhiteFiber) with its own filings; use Bit Digital's segment, not WhiteFiber's.
