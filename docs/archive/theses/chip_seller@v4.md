# Thesis: chip seller (chip_seller@v4)

## Thesis

Chip sellers are paid by the buildout. Revenue exposure is the measure that matters: the data-center or AI line of revenue is the hyperscalers' capex arriving. Capex allocation does not matter here, with one exception, Intel, whose capex is fabs and mostly not AI either. Project-level investment matters where a seller builds a fab or commits capital to a customer or partner (Nvidia's investments in Intel, Groq and OpenAI, Intel's fabs), and plans versus reality matters only for Intel's capex guidance. The signal to watch is the data-center revenue line and its growth, read against the hyperscalers' capex in the same period. The scale as of the latest filings: Nvidia's Data Center line was $193.7 billion in fiscal 2026 (year to January 25, 2026), up 68%; AMD's Data Center segment $16.6 billion in 2025, up 32%; Marvell's data center end market $6.1 billion in fiscal 2026, up 46%; Broadcom's AI semiconductor revenue grew 74% in its fourth quarter of fiscal 2025. TrendForce put Nvidia at about 70% of the AI chip market in 2025 and expects custom ASIC shipments to pass GPU shipments by 2026, which is why the custom-silicon sellers (Broadcom, Marvell) sit here with Nvidia.

## Where the AI money shows up

1. **Segment or market revenue** (`facts.segment_revenue`): the data-center or AI line. Nvidia "Data Center" (revenue by market platform), AMD "Data Center" segment, Broadcom "Semiconductor solutions" (AI semiconductor revenue is stated in releases and calls), Marvell "Data center" end market, Intel "Data Center and AI", Monolithic Power "Enterprise Data", Texas Instruments "Data center" (call only). This is the seller side of the buyers' capex: what hyperscalers paid them.
2. **Their own capex** (`values.capex`) is not AI spend in the ledger's sense. Nvidia's was $6.1 billion against $193.7 billion of Data Center revenue. Intel's fabs are the exception that proves it: $17.7 billion gross capex, mostly not AI.
3. **What the company says** (`ai_spend`): AI revenue figures (Broadcom's AI semiconductor revenue, TI's data center line), AI-attributed R&D, and capital commitments to counterparties (Nvidia's $17.5 billion of private investments, $13 billion Groq licence, $5 billion Intel stake, pending OpenAI agreement).
4. **Customer concentration** is a reliability signal, not an input: Astera Labs one customer over 70%; Credo top 10 about 90%; Broadcom top five end customers about 40%.

## How to attribute

Capex:

- `none` unless the company states an AI amount or share of its own capex (rare: a GPU cluster for internal AI research, Nvidia's "DGX Cloud"). Say in `basis` that a chip seller's capex funds fabs and test, not AI infrastructure.

Opex:

- `stated_share` or `stated_amount` only; otherwise `qualitative` when R&D is said to be AI-driven, else `none`.

Record the seller-side signal in `basis` as before: the data-center segment revenue for the year, cited, so the attribution shows what was received from AI buyers even when nothing is attributed. The revenue field below is where that signal becomes a row.

Revenue, in order of hardness. One method per company across years: a series is comparable only when every year uses the same line. When a stated AI figure exists for one year and the segment line for every year, keep the `segment` series and put the stated figure in that year's `basis` (AMD: the Data Center segment every year; the "more than $5 billion of Instinct revenue" in 2024 goes in the basis, not in `ai_amount`). Switch the whole series to the stated figure only when it is stated for every year.


- `stated_share`: the company states AI's share of revenue; confidence ≤ 0.9. None of Astera Labs, Credo, SiTime or Rambus states one as of 2026-10-29; do not invent one from a product description.
- `stated_amount`: the company states AI revenue for the year. Broadcom states "AI semiconductor revenue" in releases and on calls; use it rather than the Semiconductor solutions segment, which includes non-AI chips. Confidence ≤ 0.9 when the amount is in a release or filing, ≤ 0.6 when it is only in call coverage. Texas Instruments' data center line (call, not 10-K): ≤ 0.7.
- `segment`: sum of the `segment_revenue` facts that are the data-center line. Nvidia "Data Center" (the market line, not the "Compute & Networking" segment), AMD "Data Center" segment, Marvell "Data center" end market: confidence ≤ 0.8, with `basis` noting that the line includes non-AI servers (AMD's includes EPYC CPUs). Intel "Data Center and AI" is mostly general-purpose Xeon: confidence ≤ 0.5 and say so. Monolithic Power "Enterprise Data": ≤ 0.6.
- `qualitative`: revenue growth attributed to AI in words only (Qualcomm, Rambus, SiTime, Arm, Credo, Astera Labs), confidence ≤ 0.3.
- `none`: Synopsys and Cadence, and any seller with no AI or data-center line; the honest answer for a design-tool vendor whose customers build AI chips.
- `whole_business`: never, even for Astera Labs or Credo; use what they state.

## Projects

A project is a fab, a packaging or test site, or a capital commitment to a named counterparty with an amount: Intel's fabs (Arizona and Oregon 18A ramp, Ohio) and the government's equity stake, Nvidia's $5 billion Intel stake, its $13 billion Groq licence and its pending OpenAI agreement, a supercomputer the company builds for itself. Words that name one: "fab", "wafer", "investment of up to", "letter of intent", "equity investment", "capacity", "campus", "gigawatts" when the seller commits to supply or fund them. Not a project: a product launch (a new GPU or accelerator generation), a design win, a supply agreement with no capital from this company, a hire.

## Traps

- Data-center revenue is not this company's AI spend. It is the buyers' spend arriving. Never put it in `ai_amount` for capex; it belongs in the revenue field.
- "Compute & Networking" (Nvidia's segment, $193,479 million in fiscal 2026) includes automotive and professional visualization; the market line "Data Center" ($193.7 billion) is the AI one. The two are close in size but not the same fact.
- Broadcom's Semiconductor solutions segment ($36,858 million) is not AI revenue; the stated AI semiconductor figure is.
- Intel's Data Center and AI segment is a data-center line, not an AI one; cap the confidence.
- A supply commitment from a customer (gigawatts of GPUs to be delivered) is the customer's project; for the seller it is backlog, not spend. Nvidia's $95.2 billion of inventory purchase and supply obligations (10-K) is its own commitment to foundries, not AI capex.
- Nvidia's $17.5 billion of private investments and the $13 billion Groq payment are investing cash flows, not capex; record them as projects with the counterparty, never in `ai_amount`.
- A "data center" end market that appears only on a call (Texas Instruments) is not in the segment note; cite the call and cap the confidence.
- Marvell consolidated its non-data-center end markets into "Communications and other" in the fourth quarter of fiscal 2026; older enterprise networking and carrier facts are not comparable to the new line.
